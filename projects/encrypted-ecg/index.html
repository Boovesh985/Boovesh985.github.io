ECG Diagnosis

A 1D-CNN that classifies heartbeats into five arrhythmia classes, explains each decision with SHAP and Grad-CAM, and runs its cloud inference on CKKS-encrypted data, so the server never sees a raw heartbeat.

The problem

Deep learning reads ECGs accurately, but it has two problems in a clinic: nobody can see why it called a beat ventricular rather than normal, and cloud analysis means sending a patient's raw cardiac signal to someone else's server.

What I built

An end-to-end pipeline on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia dataset (109,446 annotated beats). A 1D-CNN learns each heartbeat, SHAP and Grad-CAM explain the result, and CKKS homomorphic encryption lets the cloud classify the beat without decrypting it. A Streamlit app walks through every step, live on Hugging Face.

Sequence

From heartbeat to encrypted diagnosis

  1. 01

    Prepare the beat

    Each 187-sample ECG segment is z-score normalised and summarised by 45 handcrafted features: P-wave, QRS, ST, T-wave and RR morphology, statistical moments, and Daubechies-4 wavelet coefficients.

  2. 02

    Train the 1D-CNN

    Three Conv1D blocks (32, 64, 128 filters) with SpatialDropout, L2 penalties, Gaussian-noise augmentation (σ = 0.2) and label smoothing, trained on 76,612 beats with Adam and early stopping.

  3. 03

    Encrypt on the device

    The CNN's 128-dimensional embedding is encrypted with CKKS through TenSEAL: polynomial modulus 8192, scale 2^40, 128-bit security, about 326 KB of ciphertext.

  4. 04

    Infer on ciphertext

    The server runs an HE-friendly MLP (128 → 256 → x² → 128 → 64 → 5) entirely in the encrypted domain. Batch-norm is folded into the dense weights and ReLU becomes a square activation.

  5. 05

    Decrypt and explain

    Only the device can decrypt the logits into one of five AAMI classes. SHAP ranks the clinical features behind the call, and Grad-CAM highlights the part of the waveform that drove it.

Decisions worth noting

Make the network encryption-friendly

CKKS can only add and multiply, so batch-norm layers were folded into the adjacent dense weights and ReLU was replaced with an x² activation.

Two explanations for every beat

SHAP DeepExplainer works on the 45 clinical features, and Grad-CAM works on the raw waveform. RR variability dominates normal beats; QRS duration dominates ventricular ones.

Regularise for real signals

Gaussian noise, SpatialDropout, L2 and label smoothing lifted accuracy past the base paper, from 94.2% to 95.66%, on a heavily imbalanced dataset.
